How a retro works

Every two weeks or so, sit together with one phone. The app walks you through these steps, one screen at a time. Skip any step you like.

·

Last time

Together

Start where you left off. Mark each open pact as kept, partly, not yet or dropped, and say how your last good time went.

·

Check-in

Each of you

Each of you picks how connected you feel, from 1 to 5. If either picks 1 or 2, the app suggests a small warm-up first.

1

Grateful

Take turns

Take turns thanking each other: the dishes after a long day, a look you still remember. Life counts too, like a new job or a great trip.

Ana saysThank you for making dinner when I got home late.
2

Unspoken

Take turns

Say what went unsaid since last time: something nice, something hard, something that slipped your mind. Start with how you felt, never with what they did.

Ben saysI felt alone when you were on your phone at dinner.
3

Repair

Take turns

Say sorry for what you'd do differently. A sentence builder helps you find the words, and the one listening gets ideas for how to reply.

Ana saysI messed up when I forgot to call. I'm sorry. Next time I'll text you.
4

Pact

Together

Turn what came up into up to three small, easy steps. Each pact has an owner and comes back at the next retro.

Ben saysAfter I get home, I will hug Ana before my phone.
5

Feeling loved

Take turns

Each of you asks: what can I do to make you feel more loved? Keep it small, and about the thought, not the price.

Ana saysPet my head when we're on the sofa.
6

Good time

Together

Plan one good time before the next retro, light, nice or big. Pick an idea or write your own.

Ben saysLight: a picnic at the lake.
7

Ask me anything

Take turns

Draw a question and ask each other: fun, deep, dreams, memories, now or silly. Or ask your own.

Ana saysIf you could move to another country tomorrow, where would you go?
·

Close

Each of you

Each of you says if you felt heard. Then a hug, and the date of your next retro.

Our story

What a retro is, and why we made lov-ed.

Questions

Do we need two phones?

No. You sit together with one phone and pass it, or keep it between you.

Is it therapy?

No. It's a structure for a good talk. When a topic keeps hurting, a couples therapist can help.

What does it keep?

Your names, typed pacts, asks and plans, and 1 to 5 scores. Never what you say.

How often?

Every two weeks works for most couples. You can pick weekly, monthly or every few days.
