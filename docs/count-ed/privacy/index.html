count-ed privacy policy

Effective: November 13, 2026

count-ed is made by factorydashed (United Arab Emirates). Contact: factorydashed@gmail.com.

The short version

count-ed keeps your counters, history and settings on your device. There is no account, and we never receive your counters, history or settings.

On Android, count-ed can send anonymous usage statistics, but only if you say yes. It is off until then. If you say yes, it sends which features get used and where people get stuck, with a random number for your phone, to PostHog in the EU. It never sends your counter names, numbers, notes or history. You can turn it off in Settings at any time.

"Report a problem" opens your own email app with a message you can read, change and send yourself. Nothing is sent unless you send it.

The free version shows ads from Google after your first three days. To do that, the app uses the internet, and Google may collect your device's advertising ID, approximate location and how you interact with ads. Where the law requires it, the app asks for your consent first, and you can change your choice in Settings.

If you remove ads, the app stops asking Google for ads.

If you choose to let a counter read Health Connect or another app's message, count-ed reads only what you picked. That data is never sent to us and never used for ads.

If you turn on Google Drive backup, count-ed saves a copy of your data in a hidden folder of your own Google Drive. We never receive it.

count-ed is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition.

What the app stores

Everything you create stays on your device: pages, counters, timers, dice, rules, history and settings. We never receive it.

The app also creates a random author ID on your device. It stays on your device unless you publish a template.

It remembers when you first opened the app, to start the ad-free days, and whether you removed ads, so the app works without a connection. It also remembers your answer about usage statistics.

It keeps a short list of what you did in the app, such as "Saved a new counter" or "Opened a timer": the last 50 actions from the past 7 days, in fixed English words. It never contains the names or numbers you typed. The list stays on your device, whether usage statistics are on or off. It leaves your device only if you tap Report a problem and send the email.

For a counter that reads from outside, count-ed also stores, on your device, the numbers it read and when, as part of that counter's history, plus when it last read and whether something went wrong. For the secret-code option it stores the code made for that counter.

Ads

The free version shows one banner, served by Google AdMob. There are no ads in your first three days, and none after you buy Remove ads.

Google may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (from which it works out an approximate location) and how you interact with ads, to serve ads and measure them. See how Google uses data from sites and apps that use its services. We don't give Google your counters, history or settings.

Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom), the app shows Google's consent form before it asks for any ad. While ads are on, you can change your choice in Settings, under Privacy choices. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android's settings.

If you buy Remove ads, the app stops asking Google for ads. Google's ad code stays in the app, unused for ads.

What a counter reads from Health Connect or another app is never passed to AdMob or to any network call, and is never used to choose or measure ads.

Usage statistics (optional)

This is off until you say yes, either on the one question count-ed asks after you save your first counter, or with the switch Share anonymous usage in Settings. If you never say yes, nothing in this section happens. It is available on Android only; the desktop and iOS versions send nothing.

  • What is sent. Which features you use (such as rules, timers, dice, table mode, full screen, sharing and Google Drive backup: whether you turned it on, off or restored, and whether it worked, never your account, files or data); which official templates you start from; the units and the style of your tiles, as categories; where you stop, such as closing an editor without saving or skipping the tips; the keys of messages the app shows you, such as an error; the app version, phone model, Android version and language. Counting itself is sent as one rough total for the day, never your numbers. If count-ed crashes, it sends the kind of error and the names of the first few places in count-ed's own code where it happened, never the error's message.
  • What is never sent. Your counter, page or rule names, your numbers, notes and history, your email address, your name or your contacts.
  • Who gets it. PostHog, which stores it in its EU cloud and handles it for us under its own terms. Each phone is identified only by a random number made on your phone. It is not your advertising ID and is not linked to you. The app does not show it to you. Like any server your phone connects to, PostHog sees the IP address the data comes from.
  • Why. To see which features are used and where people get stuck, so we can improve count-ed. The legal basis is your consent.
  • How long. Up to 12 months, then it is deleted.
  • Turning it off. In Settings, turn off Share anonymous usage. The app stops sending at once and forgets the random number, so a later yes starts a new one. Data already sent is deleted when its 12 months end. What's shared, in Settings, lists what is sent.
  • Deleting it. Email factorydashed@gmail.com. Because the random number is not tied to you or shown to you, we may not be able to tell which data is yours; in that case it is deleted when its 12 months end.

Report a problem

In Settings, under Help, Report a problem opens your own email app with a message addressed to factorydashed@gmail.com. Before it opens, count-ed shows you what the message will contain. It has room for you to write what happened, then the app version, your phone model and Android version, the language and the theme, and your last actions from the list described under What the app stores (up to 50, from the past 7 days). It never contains names or numbers you typed. You can change or delete anything, and nothing is sent until you send the email yourself. We use it to look into your problem.

Purchases

Remove ads is a one-time purchase handled by Google Play. We don't see your card details. The app only asks Google Play whether you bought Remove ads, and keeps the answer on your device, so a reinstall gets the purchase back.

Health Connect

Health Connect is Android's own store of health and fitness data. Letting a counter read from it is optional and off until you set it up.

  • What count-ed can read. Only the kinds of data you pick, one counter at a time: steps, exercise minutes, active calories, calories eaten, protein, water, sleep, weight, blood glucose and heart rate. Android asks you before count-ed can read each kind, and you can take access back at any time in Health Connect.
  • What it does with it. It turns the reading into the counter's number, so goals, streaks, charts, formulas and rules work on it. It also fills the counter's history with up to the last 30 days when you first connect it. It reads when you open count-ed and, if you allow background reading, about every 15 minutes while count-ed is closed.
  • What it never does. It never writes to Health Connect, never sends what it reads to us or to anyone else, and never uses it for ads, credit or lending decisions, employment or insurance decisions.
  • Your phone's backup. The numbers become part of count-ed's data on your device, so your phone's own backup can include them.

Numbers from other apps

A counter can accept numbers from other apps on your phone, such as Tasker. Each such counter has its own secret code. count-ed ignores any message without the exact code, for the exact counter name, and with a sensible number. Nothing is sent to us. Anyone or any app that knows a counter's code can change that counter, so treat it like a password and make a new one if it leaks.

When your data leaves the app

Your counters, history and settings leave the app only when you, or your device settings, make it happen:

  • Device backup. If Android backup is on, your device may include count-ed's data in your Google backup, including numbers read from Health Connect or another app. Google handles that backup under its own terms. Secret codes are not included.
  • Google Drive backup. Only if you turn it on in Settings. count-ed then saves a copy of your counters, history and settings, including numbers read from Health Connect or another app, to a hidden app folder of your own Google Drive, once a day and when you tap Back up now. We never receive it: it goes from your phone to your Google account. Google holds it under its own terms and it uses your Google storage. Secret codes and your Remove ads purchase are not included. To stop, turn it off in Settings under Google Drive backup; you can delete the copies there too.
  • Sharing. If you share a template by link, QR code or file, or export a CSV, it goes wherever you send it. Shared templates contain the setup, not your values or history. CSV exports contain your values and history.

Separately, the ad data described under Ads goes to Google when ads are shown.

Keeping your data safe

Your data is kept in the app's private storage on your device, protected by Android. We run no server that holds your counters, so there is no copy of them with us to lose or leak. Ad traffic to Google, Google Drive backup and usage statistics to PostHog are sent over encrypted connections.

How long data is kept, and deleting it

Your data stays on your device until you delete it or remove the app. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes it from the device. Data you read into count-ed from Health Connect goes with it; what's in Health Connect itself is yours to manage there. Device backups follow your Google account settings. To delete Google Drive backup copies, open Settings, then Google Drive backup, then Turn off, and choose to also delete them. Or open Google Drive settings, then Manage apps, and delete the hidden app data for count-ed.

We hold none of your counters, history or settings, so there is nothing of those to delete on our side. If you said yes to usage statistics, PostHog holds that data for up to 12 months; see Usage statistics (optional) for how to turn it off and ask for deletion. Google holds the ad data described above, under its own policy.

Children

count-ed is a general-audience app that anyone can use. It isn't directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data. We collect no data from children ourselves: usage statistics are off until someone says yes, and the ad data described above is collected by Google.

Changes

count-ed will keep changing. In the future we may add features that store or sync data outside your device, such as accounts, and we may change how the app is paid for, including its prices, ads, paid features or subscriptions. We may also change which anonymous usage statistics are sent.

If a change means your data is collected, stored or shared in a new way, we'll update this page and its effective date before that version ships, and we'll ask for your agreement where the law or Google's rules require it. Usage statistics stay off until you say yes. Data read from Health Connect stays within the limits above unless you agree otherwise.
